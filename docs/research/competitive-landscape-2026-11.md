## How to read this note

- **Scope.** Products that a person or company in construction uses to find work, find people, buy and sell, prove trust, run a project, learn or meet, in every region, plus the regulation that shaped them. Regional sections are ordered North America, Latin America, Europe, Middle East and Africa, South and East Asia, Oceania, and historical context for the former CIS region.
- **Evidence levels.** A plain link is a primary source (the vendor's own help page, a regulator, a statute or a court filing). *(secondary)* marks press, analysts, comparison sites and aggregators. *(user reports)* marks reviews and forum posts: they show what people complain about, not how often it happens. *(vendor claim)* marks numbers a company states about itself. Prices change often and many vendors do not publish them; every number is indicative.
- **Verdicts.** **Adopt** (take the idea as it is), **Adapt** (take it, changed to fit Trustwork's rules), **Learn** (understand why it works or fails), **Avoid** (do not build this). Every idea marked for Trustwork is **Proposed** until a work package or ADR accepts it.
- **No copying.** This note describes what products do in our own words. It copies no text, screens or designs, and nothing here endorses a vendor or implies a partnership. Russian providers are never partners (owner rule); they appear only as historical context.
- **Not legal advice.** Every legal point is a question for a hub operator's counsel.

## Executive summary

Across five continents the same few business models repeat, and the same complaints follow them:

1. **Selling access to a customer's request.** Pay per lead (Thumbtack, HomeAdvisor and Angi Leads in the US; Bark and Rated People in the UK; Check24 in Germany; ProntoPro in Italy; Cronoshare in Spain; GetNinjas in Brazil; Oferteo in Poland; ServiceSeeking in Australia) is the dominant model for private-client work. It produced the single strongest regulatory signal in this market: the US Federal Trade Commission's final order requiring HomeAdvisor to pay up to $7.2 million for misrepresenting the quality and source of its leads ([FTC, April 2023](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/04/ftc-approves-final-order-against-homeadvisor-inc-deceptively-marketing-its-leads-home-improvement)). Professionals in every country complain about paying for requests that were shared with many competitors, never answered, or not real.
2. **Selling trust as a membership.** Vetting directories (Checkatrade, TrustMark-registered schemes and Which? Trusted Traders in the UK; hipages in Australia; Angi's "certified pros" in the US) charge the people they vet. Regulators punished the words and the subscription mechanics, not the vetting: Vermont settled with Angi over calling registered contractors "certified" ([WAMC, 2025-10-13](https://www.wamc.org/news/2025-10-13/settlement-reached-over-angi-marketing-practices-in-vermont) *(secondary)*), and Australia's ACCC accepted an undertaking from hipages over undisclosed auto-renewal and early-termination fees ([ACCC, May 2023](https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/tradie-platform-hipages-rectifies-subscription-trap-issues)).
3. **Selling software by size, then raising the price.** Project-management suites (Procore by construction volume; Buildertrend, PlanRadar, Fieldwire and Jobber by seat or tier) are loved for daily work and resented at renewal; renewal increases and contract lock-in are the most repeated complaint *(user reports)*.
4. **Charging the subcontractor to be qualified for each client.** Prequalification networks (ISNetworld, Avetta in North America; Constructionline in the UK; Tilaajavastuu's Reliable Partner in Finland) are valuable to buyers and a recurring tax on small firms *(secondary)*.
5. **Selling project intelligence that is mostly public.** Dodge and ConstructConnect in the US, EstimateOne in Australia and tender aggregators everywhere repackage permits and public tenders; their complaints are about auto-renewing contracts, not data quality *(user reports)*.
6. **Content and community as the front door.** The products people love to *visit* (Houzz, Korea's Ohouse, France's Batiweb, trade media) win with content, photos and community, then monetise through advertising, commerce or lead sales. Ohouse reached its first annual operating profit with a content, community and commerce loop ([Seoulz](https://www.seoulz.com/ohouse-the-rise-of-the-k-lifestyle-giant/) *(secondary)*).
7. **Materials marketplaces with financing** are the fastest-growing segment in emerging markets: Infra.Market and Zetwerk in India, BRKZ in Saudi Arabia, Tul and Construex in Latin America, Jumba and CutStruct in Africa. They combine procurement, logistics and trade credit, and they hold money or inventory, which Trustwork will not do.
8. **Public registers are becoming machine-readable** (South Africa's CIDB register, Kenya's NCA register, NSW licence checks, Brazil's PNCP, the UK Central Digital Platform, US SAM.gov), and governments increasingly expect platforms to check them.
9. **AI agents arrived inside the incumbents in 2025.** Procore's Helix added agent building and a developer studio for agents across platforms, including MCP ([Business Wire, 2025-10-15](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251015796723/en/Procore-Advances-the-Future-of-Construction-with-New-AI-Innovations-at-Groundbreak-2025)); Bluebeam's top tier exposes MCP (see the [market scan](market-and-ideas-2026-10.md#5-construction-platforms-and-marketplaces)). Agents work *for the software vendor's customer inside one project system*; none represents an individual across the industry.
10. **Regulation converged on five rules** that Trustwork already holds or must make explicit: do not misrepresent leads; do not fake or suppress reviews (US FTC rule effective 2024-10-21, UK DMCC Act from 2025-04-06); explain ranking and say whether payment affects it (EU platform-to-business regulation); know the business customer who sells to consumers (EU Digital Services Act, Article 30); make subscriptions as easy to leave as to join and disclose renewals (ACCC v. hipages, FTC v. JustAnswer filed January 2026).

**What this means for Trustwork.** No product found in any region combines (a) a person's own plan turned into consultations and scoped tasks, (b) an agent that represents the person under readable mandates, (c) evidence-based reputation the participant owns and carries across borders, (d) honest economics where nobody pays for a lead or for rank, and (e) an open engine anyone can run for a country or city. The top ideas below are mostly **rules and guarantees** that competitors' complaints show people want, plus a handful of capabilities that only exist in one country today and that every hub could use. The [ranked list](#ideas-for-trustwork-ranked-by-impact) maps each to an existing or a new work package.

## Latin America

## Europe

### Pan-European rules that shape every European hub

| Rule | What it requires | Trustwork |
|---|---|---|
| Digital Services Act, Article 30 ("know your business customer") | Marketplaces that let consumers contract with traders must collect and make best efforts to verify trader identity and contact details before the trader can sell, and show whether a seller is a trader; applies since 2024-02-17 ([Freshfields](https://technologyquotient.freshfields.com/post/102lx12/dsa-decoded-9-the-dsa-and-online-marketplaces) *(secondary)*) | Already aligned with verification recipes; add trader status display and traceability fields to the EU layer ([idea 9](#ideas-for-trustwork-ranked-by-impact)) |
| Platform-to-business regulation 2019/1150, Article 5 | Intermediaries must describe the main ranking parameters and say whether and how payment influences ranking ([Noerr](https://www.noerr.com/en/insights/the-p2b-regulation) *(secondary)*); proposals in the digital omnibus may fold these duties into other acts *(secondary)* | Publish a ranking statement per surface generated from configuration ([idea 5](#ideas-for-trustwork-ranked-by-impact)) |
| AI Act, Article 50 | Disclosure when people interact with an AI system applies from 2026-08-02; machine-readable marking of synthetic content has a grace period for systems already on the market, reportedly to 2026-12-02 ([Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/alerts-technology-dpc-eu-ai-act-transparency-obligations-now-in-force) *(secondary)*) | Agent disclosure (HUB-005) and provenance labels (PROV-001) are mandatory, not optional, in EU hubs |
| eIDAS 2 and the EU Digital Identity Wallet | Member states must offer certified wallets by late 2026 (24 December 2026 per Implementing Regulation 2025/848 as reported); private relying parties that must use strong authentication accept them about a year later ([Fintech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/07/13/eidas-2-0-deadlines-loom-two-tracks-one-trust-chain/) *(secondary)*) | Wallet-based identity and attribute verification adapter ([idea 12](#ideas-for-trustwork-ranked-by-impact)) |
| E-invoicing: EN 16931, national mandates, VAT in the Digital Age | Germany requires every business to be able to receive EN 16931 e-invoices (XRechnung, ZUGFeRD) since 2025-01-01, with issuing obligations phasing in by 2028; EU cross-border digital reporting from July 2030 ([VATit](https://vatit.com/e-invoicing-guide/germany/) *(secondary)*) | E-invoice format and network adapters ([idea 14](#ideas-for-trustwork-ranked-by-impact)) |

## What no competitor does

1. Start from the person's own **plan** and turn each step into the right object (consultation, scoped task, RFQ, purchase, permit reminder) while the person keeps every decision.
2. Give each person **one agent** that represents them across the industry under readable, revocable mandates, with full parity between the agent and the interface ([ADR 010 decision 23](../adr/010-gathering-point-hubs-agents-and-mesh.md)).
3. Keep **money away from rank** everywhere at once: no lead fees, no paid placement in directories, matching, search or recommendations.
4. Let every participant **build and carry** their reputation as signed credentials across hubs and borders.
5. Label **sourced, claimed and verified** facts differently, including official registers, rather than replacing them with a proprietary score.
6. Run as **free software** anyone can operate for a country or city, connected in one mesh, with country law as configuration.
