# Peru: deep dive and launch plan

Rank 1 of 10 (score 85, estimate, medium uncertainty; [scorecard](../../research/top-10-launch-countries-2026-11.md#scorecard)). Status: **Proposed** playbook. Nothing here is implemented or legal advice; every legal point is a lead for counsel. Sources were accessed on 2026-10-31 unless stated; *(secondary)* marks press, law-firm notes and aggregators. Read the [generic playbook](../README.md) first: this page gives only what is specific to Peru.

## Summary

Peru is the best first launch on this list because three things meet: **most homes are built by their owners with master builders (maestros de obra), outside formal contracts; construction grew strongly in 2025; and the public data to build honest sourced pages is unusually good** (a downloadable taxpayer list, a state supplier register that records each works contractor's maximum contracting capacity, and procurement data published daily in the Open Contracting Data Standard). Spanish is a live interface language, Yape and Plin made instant phone payments ordinary, and cement makers and home-improvement chains already run large communities and training for master builders: natural partners and knowledge sources for plan-step guidance. The main risks are political instability, extortion against construction, and strict consent rules for commercial messages.

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Suggested first territory | Lima Metropolitana (with Callao), then Arequipa, Trujillo and Piura as city hubs |
| Languages | Spanish (`es`, live) as default; Quechua as a later community language (no package yet) |
| Currency, time zone | PEN; `America/Lima` |
| Country pack | Proposed CTRY-PE (generic ISO profile with local additions until then) |
| Suggested first outreach mode after counsel | `strict` |
| Fastest growth path | Partners (cement makers' master-builder communities, the construction chamber, the engineers' college, the national construction training body), content and plan guides for self-builders |

**Segments.** (1) *Self-built and progressive housing* in peri-urban districts: a family builds floor by floor over years with a maestro de obra, buying cement and steel at hardware stores; the largest segment by count and the one where Trustwork helps most. (2) *Formal residential* by developers in Lima and the large cities, including social housing programmes. (3) *Public works* at national, regional and municipal level through the state procurement system. (4) *Commercial and industrial* (warehouses, retail, mining-related works in the south). (5) *Reconstruction* after El Niño floods on the north coast.

## 2. Pain points

| Who | Pain | What Trustwork offers |
|---|---|---|
| Homeowners and self-builders | No plan, no drawings, no structural advice; buying materials without knowing quantities; paying a maestro by the week with no record; earthquake risk from poor reinforcement and concrete | A plan from the project idea (stages, trades, materials, permits, budget ranges), progressive building plans (PLAN-004), plan-step guides from licensed sources and partners, consultations with engineers, a record of tasks and payments |
| Maestros de obra and crews | Work comes only by word of mouth; skills and certificates are invisible; no portfolio | A personal profile for life, a portfolio confirmed by real clients, worker certificates from the national construction training body as sourced credentials (REP-003) |
| Small contractors | Hard to show capacity to private clients; tender documents complex | Sourced company page from public registers, claimable; procurement opportunities from OCDS; quotes that cannot be changed after sending |
| Suppliers and hardware stores | Compete on price only; training they run is not connected to sales | Manufacturer and supplier pages with guides and training mapped to plan steps, never paid ranking |
| Public buyers | Fragmented supplier knowledge; disputes on execution | Public opportunity data with sourced track records; nothing submitted to the state system by the engine |

## 4. Culture and trust

- **How people find a builder.** Through family, neighbours and the maestro who built a nearby house; hardware store counters are informal hubs where maestros are recommended. Trust is personal and local.
- **Messaging.** Facebook (24.7 million ad reach) and TikTok are near universal among adults; WhatsApp is the everyday business channel (DataReportal does not report WhatsApp for Peru) ([DataReportal Digital 2026 Peru](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-peru)). Internet users: 28.4 million, 82 percent of the population; median mobile download speed 34.71 Mbps.
- **Languages and literacy.** Spanish everywhere in business; Quechua and Aymara are official where they predominate. Adult literacy 93.7 percent (World Bank WDI, 2024). Voice notes are common for people who prefer not to write.
- **Gender and inclusion.** Construction labour is mostly male; women often manage household building budgets and decisions. Design the homeowner journey for the person who manages the money, and keep any gender data optional and aggregate.
- **Calendar.** National holidays include Holy Week, 28 and 29 July (Fiestas Patrias), 30 August, 8 October, 1 November, 8 and 25 December (to be sourced in CTRY-PE). Many families build in the dry season and around year-end bonuses (gratificaciones in July and December). On the north coast, El Niño rains (roughly December to April) stop work.
- **Trust risks.** Extortion against construction ("construcción civil" gangs) is reported in Lima and the north: public pages must never expose active site addresses, start dates or contract values that help extortion (ADDR-001; coarse locations only).

## 6. Payments

Trustwork never holds money. Rails a hub could use through licensed providers (PAY-001, PAY-003):

| Rail | Notes |
|---|---|
| Yape and Plin (phone-number transfers) | Interoperable phone wallets of the largest banks; the everyday way to pay a maestro (interoperability and volumes to be sourced in CTRY-PE) |
| Cards | Through licensed acquirers and gateways (candidates to evaluate: Niubiz, Izipay, Culqi, Mercado Pago); none assumed |
| Bank transfers (CCE) | Interbank transfers |

Use payments only when the hub charges for something (consultations, courses, event tickets). A self-builder paying a maestro outside the platform records the payment as a private record; no reputation comes from payment.

## 8. Infrastructure realities

- Mobile-first: 37.5 million cellular connections (108 percent); 82 percent online; fixed broadband concentrated in cities ([DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-peru)).
- In the highlands and Amazon regions connectivity is patchy: offline drafts and low-bandwidth pages (MOB-001) matter for site evidence.
- WhatsApp is the expected channel for notifications; consented WhatsApp and SMS through CHAN-001 only.
- Addresses in self-built districts are often "manzana and lote" (block and lot) descriptions; use coarse public locations (ADDR-001).

## 9. Partners to approach (kinds only; no outreach was made)

- The national construction chamber and regional chambers (market data, events, member announcements).
- The engineers' and architects' colleges (verification help, expert consultations, guides review).
- The national construction training body (worker certificates as credentials, course content under licence).
- Cement makers' and retail chains' master-builder programmes (knowledge partners; labelled content; never paid ranking).
- Universities with civil engineering faculties (student volunteers for guides, research with consent).
- Housing and microfinance programmes for progressive housing (development banks such as the IDB, non-profits working on incremental housing).

### Agent team tasks specific to Peru

- **Discovery (Scout, Collector, Resolver, Enricher, Page assembler, Watcher):** import the SUNAT taxpayer list filtered to construction activity codes (to be defined in CTRY-PE), link to RNP records by RUC only (never by name alone), enrich with the company's own website within robots and terms, assemble sourced pages for companies (not for natural persons), watch status changes (inactive taxpayers unpublish after staff review).
- **Editorial (Scout, Writer, Translator, Curator):** weekly news from INEI bulletins, the construction chamber, OECE and the housing ministry; a monthly magazine on self-building safety; Quechua summaries only after a native reviewer is in place.
- **Procurement (PROC-003):** daily OCDS import of works tenders into the needs board, labelled "public procurement notice".
- **Claims:** claim assistant checks RUC status, RNP record, domain e-mail and screening; a person decides.
- **Outreach:** none in `strict` mode. Growth through partners and the "Is this your company? Claim it" banner.
