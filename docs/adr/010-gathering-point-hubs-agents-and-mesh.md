# ADR 010 — Gathering point, hubs, representative agents and the worldwide mesh

Date: 2026-10-30. Status: **owner decisions recorded by the integrator 2026-10-30** (owner messages of 2026-10-30, author Dmitry Parshkov, repository owner). The decisions below are product direction and requirements. Every detailed design that implements them (the [engine handbook](../engine/README.md), [ADR 011](011-federation-mesh.md)) is **proposed** until it is reviewed and accepted task by task.

## Owner decisions (2026-10-30)

1. **The engine is a gathering point for the construction industry.** People come to read topical news and articles, to attend and to host events, to find partners, to match clients with contractors, and to keep their own agents that represent them in the industry. The goal is an engine that anyone can set up for their country or their city, that attracts an audience and partners, and that raises the quality of communication and of construction itself.
2. **Events are a public section.** Participants attend events and host their own. The public navigation already carries "Events" between "Articles" and "Companies" (`publicNav` in the locale files, checked by `scripts/validate-docs.mjs`); this decision records it and supersedes the "exact seven sections" wording of R15 and of the information architecture.
3. **Partners and matching are first-class.** Finding partners and matching clients (needs) with contractors and suppliers (offers) is a core journey, not a side effect of procurement.
4. **Representative agents.** Each participant keeps an autonomous agent that represents them in the industry and works under their direction: it looks for partners, presents their services, negotiates and pursues their goals. This extends R09–R11 without contradicting them: every person still has **one** assistant surface; the agent acts only within structured, revocable mandates; a company is represented by the agents of its authorized people under company-granted mandates; there is no multi-agent management UI. The owner later decided that a company also has a standing representative (decision 9 below).
5. **Hubs and hub operators.** The engine runs as hubs. A hub serves one country, or a region or city inside it. A hub operator is whoever chooses to develop the product for that territory. The operator, complying with local law, configures everything the territory needs: information sources, counterparty verification against local registers, sanctions screening, legal documents, payments, electronic signatures, taxonomy, editorial team and the agent policy. The engine supplies the switches, adapters, audit and guard rails; the operator supplies the legal judgement and the local connections.
6. **Country specifics are designed in.** Countries differ in company registers and identifiers, construction licensing, building codes and standards, public procurement, taxes and electronic invoicing, electronic signatures, payment rails, calendars, languages and scripts, privacy and content law. These differences live in country packs (sourced facts, reviewed in the repository) and in hub configuration (operator decisions, versioned in the database), never in hard-coded engine logic.
7. **One worldwide mesh.** Every deployment of the engine can join one network. Someone who opens the engine for a country and someone who opens it separately for a city in that country complement each other: their public content, directories and events flow between them, their participants can deal with each other, and together the deployments form a mesh that covers the world. The architecture, protocol and trust rules for this are [ADR 011](011-federation-mesh.md).
8. **Complete, friendly documentation and an open invitation.** The repository carries a detailed handbook of everything the engine does and is designed to do, and a README that explains to the community, warmly and clearly, what we build and why, and invites people to share and develop the platform and the engine.

## Owner answers of 2026-10-30 (second message)

9. **A company has a standing representative** (owner decision). Besides the agents of its people, a company may enable one **company representative** that keeps representing it when employees change. It always has a named **accountable person** among the company's current members with authority, acts only under company mandates, never exceeds the company's current grants, labels every action, and suspends automatically when no accountable person or no confirmed mandate remains. One per company, configured on the company page; still no multi-agent management UI. Design: [Your agent](../engine/agents.md#company-representation).
10. **Default federation data licences** — delegated by the owner to the integrator; decided by the integrator on 2026-10-30 as recorded in [Federation](../engine/federation.md#data-licences). Hub operators may choose stricter licences for their node; they cannot loosen privacy rules.
11. **Company claim and verification evidence** — delegated by the owner to the integrator; decided by the integrator on 2026-10-30 as the reference recipes in [Hub administration](../engine/hub-administration.md#reference-recipes-for-the-example-countries). Country register and signature providers named there are candidates until confirmed against official sources in the country packs.
12. **Publication of the open-source engine** (O19). The engine is published as `Contribuddha/trustwork` with one clean exported commit produced by `scripts/export-open-source.mjs`. Security reports go through the channels in [SECURITY.md](../../SECURITY.md). The licence and the sanctions notice are as in `LICENSE` and `NOTICE`.
13. **Attribution** (owner, 2026-10-30). The README and other appropriate places state that the engine is created and maintained by Contribuddha ([contribuddha.com](https://contribuddha.com)), to attract like-minded contributors and clients. Attribution is not a copyright claim: the copyright line stays as in `NOTICE`.
14. **Trustwork everywhere; a Russian-language name only in the Russian interface** (owner, 2026-10-31; supersedes ADR 009 decision 5 on localized lettering). The product, the engine, the public site, the workspace, the documentation and the hosted preview show Trustwork in every language. Only a visitor who chose the Russian interface sees the Russian-language name (the name, its stacked two-word lettering and the copy that embeds the name), through the overlay in `apps/web/brand`. A deployment can turn that off with `NEXT_PUBLIC_TRUSTWORK_BRAND=none` ([BRAND.md](../oss/BRAND.md)).
15. **One repository, developed in the open** (owner, 2026-10-31). Development continues directly in this repository; the earlier private working repository and its export tooling are retired, and its internal records are kept in an offline backup. `docs/engine/work-packages.json` is the source of truth for work, edited in pull requests and validated by `npm run check`; every change lands through a pull request with passing checks.
16. **A flexible engine; the reference hub is in the United States** (owner, 2026-10-31). Trustwork must be quick and comfortable to configure for any country and city: country behaviour is data (country packs, hub configuration, the setup wizard) or an adapter, never engine code. The project's own reference and demo deployment is a United States hub: English by default, USD, US states, EIN and state registrations, US company forms, state contractor licensing, state sales tax, SAM.gov and state procurement portals, ESIGN/UETA electronic signatures, card and ACH payments, US state privacy laws, US federal holidays, and Spanish as the second language (owner confirmed). The Uzbekistan, Georgia, Armenia and Azerbaijan packs remain as examples of other countries. Work packages INTL-US, DEMO-US and LANG-ES carry this out.

## Owner decisions of 2026-10-31 (product scope)

17. **From plan to people; a platform for sharing experience** (owner, 2026-10-31). Anyone can launch a project (for example building a house) and their agent turns it into a clear plan: phases, tasks, required expertise and trades, materials, permits and inspections, budget ranges, timeline, risks and decision points. Plan items become requests to platform participants for **consultations** (advice sessions, reviews of drawings or quotes, site visits) and for **execution** (scoped tasks for contractors and trades). Each side's agent automates matching, quoting, scheduling, negotiation within its mandates, progress tracking and reporting. With the owner's consent, completed projects become shared knowledge (diaries, lessons learned, checklists, templates, questions and answers, mentoring), and experts earn reputation and income for consultations and mentoring. Private plan data (budgets, floors, addresses, negotiations) stays private unless the owner publishes a redacted version.
18. **Every participant builds and confirms their own reputation** (owner, 2026-10-31). Reputation is not the platform watching people; it is a toolkit each participant uses: verified identity, licences, insurance and certificates; a portfolio of completed work backed by evidence and client confirmations; confirmations from counterparties tied to real transactions, consultations and tasks; knowledge contributions (articles, answers, consultations, mentoring, courses taught); timeliness signals; a portable, verifiable reputation passport (verifiable credentials issued by the hub) that works across hubs; agent coaching on what would strengthen the profile; and fair dispute and appeal paths. Every signal is explainable and evidence-based; reputation is never for sale and payment never changes it.
19. **Manufacturer and supplier knowledge matched to every plan step** (owner, 2026-10-31). Within the hub's legal basis and each source's licence and robots rules, the editorial office discovers manufacturers and suppliers, builds sourced company pages from their public sites and indexes not only catalogues and materials but also their training, guides, technical articles and events. When a participant works on a project, the system recommends for each plan step the relevant materials and products, articles and guides, training and courses, events, consultants and contacts. Sourced pages are labelled as sourced until the company claims them; recommendations are explained (why this item for this step), never paid for, and sponsored content, if a hub allows it, is labelled and kept out of ranking.
20. **Trustwork never holds money** (owner, 2026-10-31). The engine and hub operators never hold, pool or move participants' funds themselves. Payments, escrow, retainage and payouts go only through licensed payment providers configured per hub; the engine records instructions, provider references, evidence and reconciliation.
21. **Commissions are hub settings** (owner, 2026-10-31). The hub administrator sets commissions and fees (for consultations, tasks, marketplace categories, events, courses and agent capacity) as versioned price records with a second-person activation, an effective date and public disclosure to participants. The engine supplies no hidden fees; payment still never changes ranking, matching, recommendations or reputation.
22. **Detailed configuration for law, agents and capabilities** (owner, 2026-10-31). The engine stays flexible so each country's law can be followed through configuration:
    - **Jurisdiction settings.** Agent disclosure wording, liability allocation for agent commitments, consent and record-keeping, limits per action type and the legal texts are per-hub (and per-jurisdiction inside a hub) settings with sourced country-pack defaults, versioned and audited.
    - **Participants configure their own agent in detail.** Goals, modules, behaviour and tone, mandates and limits per action, budgets, schedules, channels, approval rules, what to share with whom, and external connections, with previews and an explanation of every setting.
    - **Administrators extend agents.** A hub administrator can add functions, tools, integrations and other capabilities to the agent through a governed extension registry: each extension declares its actions, inputs, data classes, side effects, external endpoints, costs and required mandate kinds, ships with a safety test suite, runs through an adapter with a fake, and is activated after review by a second person. Extensions may add actions; they can never bypass mandates, budgets, kill switches, privacy rules, the restricted-places list or the rule that untrusted text never issues commands.
23. **The agent has the whole system; the interface has it too** (owner, 2026-10-31). Everything in the product revolves around the person's own agent, their representative. Every participant-facing capability of the system is available through that agent, and every one is also available directly in the interface: full two-way parity. All modules register their operations in one capability registry (read, draft or commit; contract-derived inputs and outputs; data classes; mandate kind; confirmation policy; meter) that the agent runtime, the person's connected AI chat over MCP and the interface all use. A generated parity report in CI shows any capability missing from either side. Commitments still need a confirmed mandate, a budget where money is involved, and the person's confirmation; chat text never authorizes.
24. **A full sanctions-compliance engine, the same as Contribuda's** (owner, 2026-10-31): "Our hub must have a full sanctions-compliance engine — the same as we have on Contribuda." Every hub refuses restricted places at every door with visitor geolocation and holds a signed-in person seen there; screens persons, companies, counterparties and payees against the baseline OFAC lists and the lists its country pack names, with human review and separation of duties; stops money and commitments while a match is open or the answer is unknown; re-screens on every list update; and keeps the records through erasure. The same day the owner decided that the engine carries **no self-detecting kill switch** (no geolocation of the running server, no phone-home); instead our own services (federation admission, the update channel and registry, the hub directory, support and hosted services) refuse restricted places on our side, the setup wizard records a deployment-location attestation, and forks in restricted places may not use the Trustwork name (`NOTICE`). Design, parity table and open questions: [ADR 014](014-sanctions-compliance-engine.md) (proposed); requirement R72; work packages SANC-001 … SANC-009.

## Owner decision of 2026-10-31: complete administrator controls

25. **The administrator controls models, prices and agent functionality completely** (owner, Dmitry Parshkov, 2026-10-31; recorded the same day). The owner's words, translated from Russian: "The administrator must have a complete set of controls over the models in the system, their prices and the agents' functionality." A hub administrator therefore gets one complete, audited control set in the hub console, in three parts, each change a versioned hub configuration record:
    - **Models.** Provider connections and write-only keys; a per-hub model catalogue (add, describe, enable, disable, retire); pinned model versions with rollback; routing per task class and per agent module with fallbacks; data-class egress rules (private data never reaches a model without an explicit allow rule); region and residency constraints; context and size limits; evaluation before activation (the agent safety suite plus a quality check on hub-defined samples); canary and staged rollout; kill switches per model and per provider; cost and latency dashboards by cohort, never per person.
    - **Prices.** Provider cost per model; the hub's price to participants per model or per capability (flat or as a markup materialized into a fixed price version); agent plans and quotas; prepaid credits that never expire silently ([ADR 012](012-integrator-decisions-2026-10-31.md) decision 33); commissions (decision 21). All are versioned price records with a second-person activation, an effective date and public disclosure.
    - **Agent functionality.** Enabling and disabling agent modules and individual capabilities per hub, per participant type and per plan; hub defaults and limits that participants may tighten but never widen; job subscriptions; the model used by each module; the governed extension registry (decision 22); kill switches (global, per module, per capability, per extension); an audit record of every change and a preview of its effect before activation.

    How it relates to earlier decisions: it completes decision 21 (commissions are hub settings) by putting model and capability prices, plans, quotas and credits under the same versioned, second-person, publicly disclosed price records; it is the administrator's side of decision 22 (detailed configuration and the governed extension registry), which it extends from extensions to the whole model and agent product; it acts on the one capability registry of decision 23, so a capability the administrator turns off disappears from the agent, the person's connected AI chat and the interface alike, and parity holds per hub; and it leaves decision 20 untouched: the administrator sets prices, but Trustwork still never holds money, and every charge, credit purchase, refund and payout goes through the hub's licensed payment provider.

    **Guard rails no administrator can switch off:** mandates and the person's own confirmation for commitments; the sanctions gates of decision 24; the restricted-places list; payment neutrality (payment never changes ranking, matching, recommendations or reputation); no impersonation of a participant by staff; and no per-person agent management by staff: administrators manage policy for everyone, never a participant's own agent, mandates, settings, goals or connections. Earlier integrator decisions on the agent product ([ADR 012](012-integrator-decisions-2026-10-31.md) decisions 23, 24, 32, 33 and 35) stay in force. Design: [keys, providers and models](../engine/hub-console/providers-and-models.md#the-administrators-complete-control-set) and [agent product administration](../engine/hub-console/user-agents-admin.md#the-complete-control-set-adr-010-decision-25); requirement R73; work packages KEYS-001, MODEL-001, LLM-001, AGT-003, AGT-004, AGT-022, AGT-023, BILL-002, BILL-003, BILL-004, HUBC-002, HUBC-005, HUBC-008 and HUBC-009.

## Reconciliation with earlier constraints

| Earlier constraint | How it is preserved |
|---|---|
| R09 one personal assistant; no agent management UI (operating agreement) | "Your agent" is the same single assistant. Goals, mandates and runs are objects on the assistant page and on the objects they serve; there is no list of agents to configure. A company has at most one standing representative, configured on the company page with an accountable person (decision 9). |
| R10 delegation only within explicit authority; R29 durable runs | Agent work, including agent-to-agent negotiation, moves only through structured platform objects (RFQ and quote versions, agreements, introductions) with digest-bound approvals, budgets and reconciliation. |
| Untrusted content never issues commands | Messages from another participant's agent are untrusted content, exactly like e-mail or files. |
| R14 payment cannot buy ranking | Matching, partner search, event listings and federation never accept payment for position. Sponsorship is labelled and does not change ranking. |
| R15 sourced ≠ claimed ≠ verified | Hub verification adds evidence types; federation carries the badge together with the node and method that granted it. |
| R28 projections | Only the `public` projection federates by default; `shared_party` data travels only between the nodes of the parties to a deal. |
| ADR-009 sanctions | Hubs and mesh nodes in restricted places cannot exist or federate; screening stays mandatory; the engine of decision 24 and ADR 014 implements it. |
| Staff never manage a participant's agent (R09, no agent management UI) | Decision 25 gives administrators complete control of the agent **product** (models, prices, modules, capabilities, extensions, kill switches) for everyone in the hub; no control reads or changes one person's agent, mandates, settings or conversations. |

## Consequences

- The specification gains requirements R32–R39 ([specification](../product/specification.md)); the information architecture lists eight public sections plus search.
- The [engine handbook](../engine/README.md) documents implemented, demo, designed and proposed functionality with an honest status for each.
- New work packages are added to the task graph (events, learning, matching, agents, hubs, verification, country pack v2, federation).
- Decision 25 adds requirement R73 and the model catalogue package MODEL-001, and amends the acceptance of the providers, gateway, agent product, billing and console packages that own the other controls.
