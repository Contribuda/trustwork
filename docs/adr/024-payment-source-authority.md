# ADR 024: Payment source authority and bilateral payment authorization

Date: 2026-11-01. Status: **Proposed**; conductor technical review allocated a bounded first-party, single-hub commercial implementation on 2026-11-01; implementation review remains pending. Owner: PAY-001 (#80), draft PR #510. This allocates no operational provider, credentials, publisher or authority to existing billing permissions. No frozen commercial or mesh contract changes here.

## Problem and existing contracts

`PaymentBindingSchema` in `packages/contracts/src/payments.ts` needs facts that the current `person`, `company` and `agreement` records do not own: responsible payment hubs, verified party countries and connected-account ownership. A locale, IP, employer, reminder `source_hub_id`, declaration or participant-supplied account ID cannot provide them. A provider-authenticated callback proves its sender, not an arbitrary engine-party association in its payload.

Existing exact sources:

- `agreement` and `quote_version`, owned by commerce, carry immutable accepted digest, offer/request versions, party IDs and quote gross amount/currency. `commercial_order` carries the original transaction reservation and accepted digest. See [immutable evidence](003-snapshots-evidence-and-authority.md), `packages/data/migrations/transactions/20261101120000_transactions.sql` and `packages/contracts/src/deals.ts`.
- `task_assignment` and `consultation_booking` pin their owning accepted records (`packages/data/src/schema/tasks.ts`, `packages/data/src/schema/consultations.ts`). Their factories are owned by AGT. Platform fee holds are not settlement reservations. No unconsumed adapter is integration.
- `provider_connection`, `hub_config_current` and immutable `hub_config_version` carry configured provider identity/capabilities and activated hub policy. `packages/data/src/repositories/providers.ts::providerConnections` and `hubs.ts::hubConfig` expose the owning commands. They do not establish participant account ownership.
- `payment_source` is an immutable runtime projection with a revocation timestamp and no production publisher. `payment_rail` is owner-only. `payments_authority`, `payments_route_available`, `payments_funds_guard`, `payments_begin` and `payments_dispatch_guard` are in `packages/data/migrations/payments/20261123100000..20261123100005`. `payment_operation.admitted_authority` records the admitting person/company epochs and membership ID. Initial settlement keeps intent -> operation -> BILL order.
- `screening_gate_check` (`screening-gates/20261103030000_screening_gates.sql`) serializes local baseline/subject/hold checks. `screening_available(clock_timestamp())` checks age after domain waits. This single-node authority is not remote federation.
- `PaymentProviders` in `apps/worker/src/payments/provider.ts` ships empty. `PaymentRuntime.receiptPool` is a separate trusted verifier boundary; ordinary app/worker SQL cannot call `payments_receipt`. No login or operational verifier is provisioned.

## Proposed first positive scope

First-party individuals, one responsible hub, one currency, one immutable commercial order and its existing BILL transaction reservation. Both parties accept the complete payment authorization. Companies, mandates, tasks/consultations, multiple local hubs and remote hubs remain unavailable until their separate owning contracts and consumed implementations exist. The operational registry remains empty even if synthetic tests pass.

A chosen **responsible payment hub** is a service/routing selection accepted by that hub, not a country or domicile fact. `payerHubId` and `payeeHubId` would reference those versioned bindings; this proposed interpretation needs review before a publisher may populate them. A **declared country** is explicitly unverified and cannot satisfy the verified-country gate. For the first individual slice, party country means the identity verifier's explicitly attested residence-country fact; provider account country is a different field. No claim about applicable law is made by either fact.

## Proposed records and limits

These proposed records are allocated for the bounded local implementation; they do not authorize operational registration. Every record has an opaque ID, schema version, canonical digest, creation time and issuer registration version. Immutable versions have separate current/revocation pointers; no historical record is overwritten. UUIDs and ISO codes use existing contracts. Digests use `trustwork-digest-1`; raw signatures use an explicitly registered algorithm/key version, never a Boolean.

| Proposed record | Required contents and owner |
|---|---|
| `payment_identity_link_version` | Exact engine person ID, issuer-owned opaque verified-subject ID, original challenge ID/digest, authenticated actor/session and epoch, provider-independent identity assurance category, explicitly attested residence country, issued/expiry times and issuer receipt digest. Only a registered identity-verification adapter can attest it; ordinary platform sign-in is insufficient. No names, email matching or government identifiers in the payment projection. |
| `payment_participant_binding_version` | Exact person and identity-link version, responsible hub ID, person's signed first-party selection/consent version, independent hub acceptance and revocation reference. Country declaration is optional and explicitly separate from verified country. The hub attests service acceptance only, never identity or residence. |
| `payment_account_evidence` | Exact connection/config digest, provider-owned account and entity references, identity-link issuer/subject correlation, challenge, evidence mode, capabilities, provider-attested account country/status, observation/expiry times, signed receipt digest. A registered provider adapter authenticates account evidence. It cannot attest hub choice or agreement consent. |
| `payment_account_binding_version` | Exact payee person + identity link + participant binding + account evidence IDs/digests, responsible hub and connection; original requester/session/epoch; distinct reviewer's session, permission/role version and exact action approval. A private opaque evidence trail proves entity identity linkage, not a matching display name. |
| `payment_authorization_version` | Exact original agreement/digest and immutable offer/request version references, original order/reservation reference, payer/payee binding versions, verified countries, account binding version, collecting hub, connection, rail, full integer amount/currency and expiry; bilateral acceptance receipts over the SAME canonical complete snapshot. Separate from the agreement and its digest. |
| `payment_source_version` | Minimal projection of an active bilateral authorization, exact source IDs/digests, registration version, issuance/expiry and revocation sequence. Unique authorization ID/version. New runtime reference required; existing one-row `payment_source` cannot silently become a mutable current-account selector. |

Technical bounds proposed for review: one canonical source/authorization payload <=16 KiB; account/identity adapter envelope <=8 KiB; opaque references <=180 ASCII characters; at most two participant bindings and one account binding; no arbitrary URLs or nested unbounded evidence. A server challenge has 256 random bits, one use, purpose and audience, expires within 10 minutes. An account/identity assertion's usable period is at most 24 hours and never exceeds the issuer's own expiry. A source expires at the earliest referenced expiry; activation never extends evidence. A bilateral authorization draft expires within 24 hours; acceptance after expiry needs a new version. These are engineering freshness/size ceilings, not legal retention periods. Retention remains the separately governed privacy policy; unknown financial evidence is not deleted or released by these timeouts.

## Exact engine-party linkage and original publisher

1. The first-party authenticated person requests a server-created challenge. The server records person ID, exact session, epoch, requested purpose, hub, registered verifier/connection audience and nonce digest. Request bodies contain no replacement party ID. Step-up/consent requirements are part of the proposed `payments.bind_account` governance command, not a chat instruction.
2. The registered identity adapter authenticates a challenge-bound verification response that links its verified subject to that engine person. This requires an explicit trusted identity-link capability; existing email/password/provider sign-in alone cannot create it. If the issuer cannot prove this linkage, the flow stays unavailable.
3. The provider adapter completes provider-authenticated account authorization on the same challenge. It reads account owner entity and country from the authenticated provider API/callback, not from echoed callback metadata. Its signed evidence must correlate that entity with the identity verifier's subject through a registered cross-issuer identity-link proof. Control of an account or knowledge of its identifier alone is insufficient. No name/email comparison is permitted. Unsupported correlation remains unavailable.
4. A narrow verifier command checks challenge purpose/audience/nonce, original actor/session/epoch, provider connection, identity link, expiry, signature and single use, then stores immutable evidence. Duplicate identical evidence replays; differing evidence on the same challenge is a conflict. No raw account/bank details enter payments.
5. The person requests the exact account binding. An independently authorized hub reviewer approves its digest. The reviewer cannot mint provider/identity evidence and cannot be the requester, source proposer, identity-link attestor's accountable operator or either contracting person. A review decision is not proof of account ownership by itself.
6. Both parties accept the exact complete `payment_authorization_version`. The acceptance command resolves each party from its own current session and referenced original agreement side; the second party cannot be selected in a request. Both acceptances bind the same digest and current source versions. A payment publisher reads these records and immutable order facts; it does not accept a caller-selected `PaymentBinding`.

Proposed `payments_publish_source(authorization_id, version)` has a fixed projection and no overrides. Original publisher authority comes from an independently activated registration record: implementation and contract version/digest, exact allowed issuer/connection/hub, allowed fact kinds, public verification key/version, evidence mode, time/size ceilings and accountable human. Proposed registration action `payments.register_source_adapter` requires a proposer with `admin.payments.adapters.propose` and a distinct reviewer with `admin.payments.adapters.activate`; neither can approve their own registration. Installation provisions only the reviewed narrow signing/verifier identity. No self-registration endpoint or app/worker grant may create registration or private signing authority.

Account review uses proposed `admin.payments.account_binding.review` and action `payments.activate_account_binding`. Participant governance uses proposed `payments.bind_account` and `payments.accept_authorization`, first-party only initially. Company support would require proposed permission `company.payments.authorize`, explicitly scoped to company ID, account binding and payment authorization IDs, maximum amount/currency, expiry and whether bilateral acceptance is allowed. A future mandate kind `payment_authorization` must carry those same exact limits and an accountable person; `company.billing.manage` is not reinterpreted as legal representation. None of this vocabulary is added to frozen capability/permission contracts by this ADR.

## Field authority matrix

| Consumed binding field | Exact authoritative evidence; who may attest |
|---|---|
| `agreementId`, `agreementDigest`, `offerVersion`, `requestVersion` | Owning immutable agreement and accepted quote/RFQ references; commercial acceptance code. Publisher only reads and compares; a reviewer cannot rewrite them. |
| `payer`, `payee` | Original agreement party IDs plus exact engine-person/verified-subject links; owning commerce proves the contracting sides, registered identity verifier proves the external identity link. Provider callback party metadata is ignored. Companies unavailable initially. |
| `amountMinor`, `currency` | Immutable quote gross and original commercial order/reservation, plus the identical bilateral payment-authorization snapshot; no new amount, conversion or fee substitution. |
| `payerHubId`, `payeeHubId` | Current participant-binding versions: exact person's first-party choice AND owning hub's explicit service acceptance. Not residence, locale, IP, employer or reminder provenance. Same hub required initially. |
| `payerCountry`, `payeeCountry` | Current signed identity-link residence-country assertion for the exact party; declaration or account country cannot substitute. Missing supported issuer fact means unavailable. |
| `collectingHubId` | Both parties' authorization of the exact collector plus its active payment configuration and own service acceptance. Initially the common responsible hub only. |
| `connectionId` | Bilateral authorization pins the connection, current `provider_connection` and active collecting-hub config/rail allow it; source account evidence belongs to this connection/config. |
| `connectedAccountRef`, `connectedAccountCountry` | Authenticated provider-owned account/entity evidence, exact verified-subject correlation, first-party payee binding and distinct independent review. Neither operator account nor caller-provided destination is admitted. |
| `rail`, `evidenceMode` | Registered adapter capabilities, current approved hub rail and bilateral authorization. Only the adapter registration determines evidence mode; synthetic assertions can never become licensed-provider evidence. |

Every field must have its required records. No fallback to another field, current membership or arbitrary selected authority is allowed.

## Local ordering, revocation, mismatch and old agreements

The publisher locks current references and original owning records in a reviewed order, computes the minimal projection, and appends audit/outbox in one transaction. Payment admission then locks current participant/identity/account/registration/source revocation references, exact actor/session/epochs, immutable agreement and current route/screening authority. Existing settlement order remains intent -> operation -> BILL. All dynamic references are pinned and reread after domain waits; expiry uses wall time. A source revoker and dispatch either serialize revoker-first (no effect) or dispatcher-first (revoker waits for the bounded final provider invocation). The executable migration must demonstrate the full lock order against each owning mutator, not merely copy this description.

Current source/key/identity/account revocation or expiry prevents a new effect. Verification records distinguish issuer-key validity at evidence creation from current authorization to dispatch. A historic terminal receipt remains eligible for reconciliation against the original operation/binding and the key valid when evidence was authenticated. Revoked or disputed evidence with an unprovable signing time is quarantined for independent resolution; it cannot release an unknown hold. A mismatched account, amount, currency, provider reference, challenge, issuer or source digest is rejected/quarantined without changing financial state. Provider disagreement never becomes proof of failure.

A changed account needs a NEW complete bilateral payment authorization. Neither published RFQ/quote bytes nor accepted agreement digests change. Old agreements with no authorization remain unavailable; no backfill or unilateral current-account state authorizes them. Parties may explicitly create a new authorization referencing an old immutable agreement only through both real current acceptances; historical acceptance is never fabricated. An unknown old operation is reconciliation-only, even if a newer authorization is accepted; uniqueness/funds guards must prevent a second charge while the old effect remains unknown.

## Remote admission: proposed protocol and unavailable implementation

A cached clear receipt plus revocation lookup is rejected: revocation can commit between lookup and effect. The first slice therefore has NO remote positive path. The following bounded reservation protocol is the concrete candidate for separate technical review; no existing mesh endpoint or provider promises it.

Each remote hub needs a durable `payment_admission` record keyed by operation ID, exact authorization digest, subject binding, local screening baseline/result epochs, provider/collector, attempt fence and a common acceptance deadline. States: `prepared`, `consumed`, `aborted`, `expired`; an additional revocation-request flag denies new preparations immediately. The coordinator has a durable decision record. These are authority reservations, not money or BILL reservations.

1. Outside any global/local authority DB lock, the collector requests independent prepare from each responsible hub, in stable hub-ID order. Each hub transaction locks its own current subject/source/screening authority, checks time and revocation, and atomically reserves the exact operation/fence. It returns its signed prepare certificate. The same local revoker lock is used: revoker-first refuses prepare; prepare-first creates a bounded reservation that the revoker must account for.
2. Prepare is a promise that the specified admission remains consumable through its deadline, not a cached observation. A revocation request immediately stops other work; for this reserved operation it completes only after a provider fence acknowledgement/abort or the acceptance deadline. This is a material proposed authority rule, not already granted by current screening contracts. If a hub's owning revocation policy cannot allow that bounded in-flight interval, it cannot support this protocol and remote payment stays unavailable.
3. Proposed maximum preparation interval: 5 seconds, no extension, with a separately verified maximum clock uncertainty of 250 ms. Collector and provider use conservative lower expiry bounds. Unbounded clock uncertainty, expired certificates, a slow prepare or a domain wait consuming the interval aborts admission. This does not free financial holds. Local DB transactions/locks end before every network call.
4. Only a registered provider adapter that offers atomic **conditional acceptance** can consume the instruction. It verifies BOTH independent hub certificates, their exact matching operation/authorization/fence/collector/provider/account/amount/currency and deadlines, then atomically stores a one-time acceptance or an abort tombstone before effect execution. Provider acceptance must fail after the earliest conservative deadline and after any acknowledged higher fence/tombstone. Ordinary idempotency keys alone do not provide this contract. A provider that cannot enforce it is unavailable for remote payment.
5. The serialization point for an admitted effect is that provider's conditional acceptance while BOTH hub promises are valid. Revoker-first at either hub prevents its prepare certificate. After prepare but before acceptance, a revoker may race an abort/fence with acceptance at the provider: abort-first denies; acceptance-first is already an in-flight operation. The revoker may not report its reserved-operation cancellation effective before fence acknowledgement or deadline. After deadline, delayed packets cannot be accepted; an already accepted operation remains reconciliation-only. Both orders and certificate expiry during waits need real protocol tests.
6. Collector crash before both prepares: no complete certificate, no provider effect; prepare reservations expire. Crash after both prepares but before send: recovery performs original-operation lookup/fence, never creates a new charge. Lost acceptance acknowledgement or split connectivity leaves the financial outcome unknown. No participant, collector or provider may infer failure from timeout. Prepared authority expires for NEW acceptance; the original financial hold survives until authenticated terminal evidence. An unknown operation cannot be replaced by a new authorization/fence that causes another effect.
7. Each hub signs only its own independently scoped current subject decision. A single local result copied into two certificates is invalid. Two local hubs would need independently scoped owning subject checks in one actual database transaction and separate hub policies; that would prove only local multi-hub behavior, not this remote protocol.

This candidate explicitly depends on reviewed revoker completion semantics, bounded clocks and conditional provider acceptance. They do not exist in the shipped engine. Until all are accepted, implemented and tested with independent authorities and partition/crash cases, different home hubs and third-hub collection remain unavailable. Frozen `trustwork-mesh/1` is untouched.

## Alternatives and consequences

- Infer authority from request, locale, IP, current employer or reminder hub: rejected; no provenance or bilateral consent.
- Treat an authenticated account callback as engine-party proof: rejected; sender authentication does not bind identity.
- Write new fields into accepted agreement/quote: rejected; destroys immutable consent. Separate payment authorization is additive and reviewable.
- Reuse billing permission or fee holds: rejected; neither proves representation nor settlement reservation.
- Remote cached screening or expiry-only retry: rejected; revocation race and duplicate financial risk.
- First-party, single-hub strict source records: selected proposal for the smallest positive engineering slice. More records and unavailable results are preferable to invented authority.
- Remote durable reservations with provider conditional acceptance: candidate only, deliberately unavailable pending explicit technical review. It trades bounded revocation completion and provider capabilities for serializable admission; unsupported hubs/providers refuse.

## Allocation and verification

PAY owns the proposed source records/projections and payment adapter boundary. Identity owns verified-subject linkage; commerce owns immutable agreement and acceptance provenance; HUB owns hub service acceptance/review permissions; SANC owns scoped screening/revoker semantics; AGT owns task/consultation factories; PROV owns provider publication. The conductor allocated additive PAY records/contracts/API/publisher, minimal explicit ADM entries and owner tests for the local individual commercial slice. Remote protocol, company and AGT source hooks require subsequent allocation. No files in those other owning implementations are changed by this proposal.

Required evidence: each field's issuer/party/audience/challenge negative cases; distinct registration/account reviewers; original publisher cannot self-authorize; raw app/worker forgery refusal; old-agreement and one-sided acceptance refusal; account-change bilateral replacement; payload bounds; source/key/receipt expiry/revocation distinctions; every owning local revoker in both orders; expired authority after waits; remote prepare/fence/revoker/clock/partition/crash tests if ever enabled; no duplicate sends or timeout-based hold release; privacy projections and no operator custody. Fake-adapter observations remain labelled synthetic. Completion of these tests is not operational provider or maintainer acceptance.
