How a hub opens and how a partner joins

The network has one door and several rooms. A hub operator, a municipality, a lender, a manufacturer, a funder, a contributor and a country-pack maintainer each join differently, but the shape is the same: a real place or a real case, a named accountable person, a clear way the engine reaches you, evidence of what you did, and a record built only from that. This chapter says what each room looks like, what every partner can count on, what we ask, and what Trustwork and Contribuda keep to themselves.

What Trustwork does itself

The engine does a small number of things and is careful not to drift beyond them. It keeps the record, gives every person one representative agent, runs the gathering point, carries each country's facts in a pack and federates public projections (chapters 1 and 8). Contribuda maintains it and gives it away under its licence, with no licence fee and no implementation fee; nobody needs Contribuda to run a hub.

What neither does: hold money, lend, insure, inspect, vote, license, build, practise law, or decide a claim. Every one of those is a partner's room, and a partner who does one of them well is not a competitor; they are a participant the record is waiting for.

The ways to join

  • As a hub operator

    You run the engine for a country, a region or a city: an association, a chamber, a publisher, a university, a training body, a cooperative, an entrepreneur or a community of engineers. The launch playbook takes you from decision and preparation to installation, lawful sourcing, the compliance gates and a soft launch with real people, before you switch to public (chapter 9). The minimum real hub is two people and the agent team. A hub can start free, charge nothing, and still be a hub. If you wish, and only then, you will be able to open the hub as a project in your personal Contribuda account and manage its team, plans and development from there; the next chapter says what that would mean and what never crosses.

  • As a civic partner

    A municipality, a county, a province, an audit office, a procurement authority or a civic body brings what it already has: a budget, a project list, a procurement feed, an oversight committee, a law that lets residents watch a work. The hub imports what is public as sourced pages before it asks anyone for anything, adds the evidence and confirmation layer, and shows the trail from budget line to building for one cycle. The partner keeps its vote, its law and its data; a vote, if the engine's module is used, is run by a neutral keeper, a municipality, an electoral institute or an independent civic body, never by an industry hub operator, and identity is proven outside the hub. What a civic partner never gets: private data of residents or companies, a say in anyone's reputation, a ranking of anyone, or a record that flatters by design. The civic ring is a direction of this book and opens only where the civic-space gate is open.

  • As a financing partner

    A licensed lender, a housing-finance institution, a microfinance body, a mortgage refinance company, an escrow provider, an insurer, an independent inspector or a valuer. You are a reader of the build file, with the borrower's consent: plan, permits, named professionals, frozen quotes, inspection gates, field evidence, acceptance, changes and defects, the contractor's record. You disburse, insure, inspect or value in your own systems and under your own licence, and the engine records the act as evidence with your name on it. The lines of chapter 10 apply in full, among them that the engine never lends or holds money. You earn a reputation from your acts like every other participant, and a stage released against bad evidence is a record too. None of this is built yet (chapter 18); it begins with one real build, in a place where a staged, inspected disbursement already exists.

  • As a knowledge partner

    A manufacturer, a supplier, a professional body, a training centre, a university or a publisher brings knowledge: technical guides, course material under a written licence, experts who answer consultations, reviewers for seed guides, events. Your material appears on the plan steps where it helps, labelled as yours, because it fits and never because you paid. Your experts earn reputation and income from the consultations they give. Sponsored content never ranks. Certificates your body issues can be recorded on the hub with the accreditation shown, and the engine never calls anything "certified by Trustwork".

  • As a funder

    A development bank, a donor, a foundation, a government programme or a company funds a hub, a country pack, a language, a module or the engine itself. What you buy is work in the open: a work package with acceptance criteria, a pull request anyone can read, a status that says Live only when it is. What you do not buy: a ranking, a data set about people, a say in reputation, a hub's silence, or exclusivity. Chapter 3 says why a single funder is a risk, and we will tell you what else funds the work.

  • As a contributor to the engine

    Developers, designers, translators, lawyers and practitioners, alone or with an AI agent you are accountable for. Work packages are public and decisions are recorded in the open; chapter 27 says how a change is made.

  • As a country-pack maintainer

    You know a country: its identifiers, registers, licensing, codes, procurement, tax, signatures, payments, holidays, and now its civic facts too, the participation statute, the oversight committee's standing, the whistle-blower protection or its absence, the civic-space rating with its date and source. Every fact needs a source and a verification level. You are the source of truth for your country's facts, and every hub in that country configures itself from them. Even one well-sourced facet helps.

What every partner can count on

  • Nothing to pay us for the engine. No licence fee, no implementation fee. A hub's income stays with its owner in its country; a hub operator's own costs, hosting, people and local adaptation, are its own. Contribuda's only part is the international layer, in a form to be decided and disclosed to both parties (chapter 7).
  • Honest status. A partnership is named only when it exists and only with your agreement. A documented API is not a partnership.
  • Evidence, not advertising. Your record on the hub is built from accepted work and confirmed acts. Every reputation snapshot shows its method and its sample size. No score is for sale, and no payment, plan, sponsorship or staff action changes a ranking.
  • Your data stays yours. Private stays private, including from hub staff, who read a private record only under a scoped case grant. Deal data travels only between the parties' nodes. Public projections are the only thing that federates by default.
  • One lawful frame. Sanctions compliance applies to every deployment and every counterparty; restricted places are never territories, hosts or markets; counsel is named on every legal step; nothing in the book or the handbook is legal advice.
  • A right of reply. A contractor, an institution or a partner can answer any observation on its page with evidence; a disputed observation stops counting until a different person decides; a fair negative record can be answered, never deleted.
  • An exit. The engine is free software. A hub operator can leave with its data; a participant can export theirs and move to another hub; a node can run without the Trustwork name. Nobody is locked in, and that is why the record can be trusted.

What we ask

A real first case, not a catalogue of possibilities: one municipality, one build, one province's project list, one course. A named person on your side who is accountable and can decide. Clear terms we can read and act within. Patience: the first integration is a manual step until the adapter has earned its place. Acceptance of the rule that evidence is published whether it flatters or not, with a right of reply, never a right of deletion. And for a civic partner, one more: that the vote, where there is one, belongs to a neutral keeper, and that no resident is ever the object of a record.

What Trustwork and Contribuda keep to themselves

A few things are not for partnership, because the record depends on their not being shared. The reputation method and its computation stay with the participant's home node; others display it with attribution. The lines of chapter 15 are not configurable by any hub, partner or funder, and neither is the outreach floor: every hub starts strict, and outreach beyond that happens only under a counsel-approved hub policy with a second person, to organisation and role addresses, never as bulk messages to private persons. Private data never leaves its node and is never a term of any agreement. The civic-space gate is to be set by a recorded decision, with its sources; a partner may ask for a review of it, not for a lower bar. The Trustwork name on a hub follows the brand rules and the charter, and the engine can always be run without it. Product decisions belong to Dmitry Parshkov as the project's owner, recorded by date; an operators' association, proposed before the fourth operator, would hold the charter and advise, while the Trustwork name and mark remain Dmitry Parshkov's. We say so now so that nobody is surprised later.

One door, several rooms, the same floor under all of them: a real case, a named person, and a record nobody can buy.
