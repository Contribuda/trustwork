The five years, year by year

Five years, told as five stations on one rising line: what the world around the engine will probably look like, with sources; what Trustwork will have built by then; and which rings it reaches. The years are the arc's shape, not promises of dates. The order is the point, and the logic behind the order is given once, here, and then kept.

The shape

Construction first, because the engine already works there and because public money is visible there. The civic ring only with a consenting municipality and a lawful operator, and only where the civic space is open enough that the people who look are safe. Standards and recognition before scale: a digital public good, a verifiable credential, a recognised certificate, an open build file. An operators' association before the fourth operator, so that the engine's voice does not belong to its maintainer. Self-funding before the tenth country, so that no single funder's delay can end it. Each year below adds a ring, and each ring is built on the courses of the year before.

Year one, 2027: home, and the loop closed once

The world. The European Union's transparency rules for AI systems apply from August 2026, so by 2027 an assistant must say it is one; the high-risk obligations for systems that affect access to essential public services and economic opportunity are set for 2 December 2027, and a tool that ranks contractors for a public tender may fall under them. Cities are letting machines pre-check building permits and keeping the decision human: Austin contracted after a pilot, Seattle's evaluation found its automated intake screening caught 87 per cent of the errors reviewers found and reviewers agreed with the automated plan review 92 per cent of the time, Toronto labels its pre-check "advisory only". And the rails are being laid: the campaign for fifty countries to deploy one component of digital public infrastructure by 2028 has passed thirty members.

Trustwork. The San Diego reference hub in production (chapter 23), English first and Spanish second: sourced news, the weekly magazine, events, courses with certificates checkable by number, profiles and company pages, plan to people for private projects, reputation without a public score, the representative agent under mandates. A read-only "works of the city" layer from the city's open data, following a public work from capital budget line to contract to field evidence, with no vote. The civic decision record written and reviewed. The application to be recognised as a digital public good prepared. The first draft of the open build-file standard, and the first conversations with licensed lenders, insurers and inspectors who ask. Nothing civic is promised to any government.

The ring. The construction core, in one country, used by real people for the first time.

Year two, 2028: three operators, the first civic loop, the first financed build

The world. The year the fifty-countries campaign set as its deadline. The European high-risk obligations in force since December, and the first conformity assessments of public-sector scoring and ranking tools. Permitting through model-based gateways becoming normal where Singapore showed the way, replacing more than twenty approval touchpoints with three submission gateways. AI assistants in government counted in registries, as Singapore began for its public officers in 2026, which is the precedent for operator agents with a named accountable person.

Trustwork. Hubs run by at least three independent operators, an association, a publisher, a university or training body, in two or three of the launch countries, each with a locally maintained country pack; the mesh federating public projections and nothing else. The first full loop of public trust with one consenting municipality; the Dominican Republic is the clearest case, where participatory budgeting is mandatory, open contracting data is daily and the country is a CoST member (chapter 20 gives the reasons): the vote held by the municipality under its own law with identity proven outside the hub, the record kept around it as chapter 4 told. The first private builds financed stage by stage by a licensed lender reading the build file, released on accepted evidence and an inspector's act, in a country where a staged, inspected disbursement already exists in law or practice (chapter 10 names them); nothing is agreed with anyone. The operators' association founded, with a charter that carries the invariants and the list of what we will never build.

The rings. The construction core in several countries; the civic ring, once; the financing ring, once.

Year three, 2029: the civic ring is a module; the engine is a recognised public good

The world. The European rules for high-risk systems embedded in products apply from August 2028. National digital public infrastructure, identity, payments and data exchange, available as adapters in most of the launch countries; the open identity platform reports agreements with up to twenty-nine countries, the Philippines among its largest deployments and Peru piloting components. Another wave of the OECD's trust survey, likely asking again whether governments kept their safeguards when they adopted AI.

Trustwork. Any hub can enable the civic ring under its country's law. The kinds of place that come next: a province with a backlog of paralysed works, under an audit office's citizen-monitor frame and with a municipal association as operator; a city with a participatory budget of twenty years' standing, a university and oversight committees with legal standing; a region whose infrastructure disclosures already flow to an independent reviewer; a county that pays contractors only after residents approve. Chapter 20 says where each of these conditions exists today, and nothing is promised to any of them. Recognition as a digital public good, or the published list of what was missing. Certificates mapped to recognition of prior learning in at least one country, with a national training authority as the assessor. Each hub's open statistics, cost ranges, completion times and defect rates by stage and building type, consented, aggregated and never exposing anyone. The first insurer pricing from evidence. The hub operating company's functions staffed by agents and people in at least five hubs. An annual delivery report for each participating municipality, written from records, with the municipality's reply beside it. Baukultur statements on public-works pages where an operator adopts the eight criteria.

The rings. The civic ring in several countries; the financing ring in a few; the learning ring recognised outside the hub for the first time.

Year four, 2030: a federation that funds itself

The world. The United Nations' Global Digital Compact, adopted in September 2024, set 2030 for its commitment to invest in digital public goods and infrastructure, and the year will review it. Europe's public authorities must bring their older high-risk systems into compliance by August 2030. AI agents in public procurement work under conformity rules. The first generation of self-builders who learned on hubs finish their houses.

Trustwork. The operators' association financially independent of any one funder, from membership fees, services and grants, holding the charter and advising on the use of the name, which remains Dmitry Parshkov's mark; Contribuda as maintainer (chapter 7). A second, independent implementation of the federation protocol by someone who is not Contribuda. The reputation passport accepted by at least one outside verifier, a lender, an insurer or a licensing board. More kinds of civic loop: a province whose national project list and audit commission are already public; a community that already puts its draft acts out for comment; a borough whose vote is already run by an independent electoral institute, when a willing one and access to its data exist. Income to hubs from commissions as settings, events, courses and services, never from ranking and never from data, and all of it belonging to the hub's owner in its country. A public failure log: projects voted and not built, disputes, revoked certificates, launch gates failed, because the credibility of the loop depends on showing what did not work.

The rings. The federation itself becomes a ring: an engine whose voice belongs to the people who run it.

Year five, 2031: an engine others run, where it is especially needed

The world. Post-disaster reconstruction and the upgrading of informal settlements increasingly run through homeowner-driven models with staged digital payments, the shape Nepal gave them after 2015, generalised. National permit systems exposing machine-readable gateways that accept staged field evidence at completion. Trust surveys measuring whether AI in government kept its safeguards.

Trustwork. Hubs in ten or more countries run by local operators under one charter. The civic ring in several municipalities per country where the law allows, and paused where it does not. The build file read by many lenders and insurers, so that a family can borrow and insure its own house stage by stage. Certificates recognised for informal workers in at least two countries. A shared library of project knowledge, with owners' consent, in every hub language, so that a self-builder in Lima or Accra reads how someone else solved the same wall. A federated delivery record that lets a resident see what their vote became, wherever the work was built. And still: no score of residents, ever; no money held; no ranking for pay; no watching of workers.

The rings. All of them, in the places where they are especially needed, run by people who live there.

Where the civic ring waits

Where civic space is closed, the construction hub runs alone and the civic ring waits. Chapter 20 names the four places and the yearly review, and says why this is a consequence of our lines, not a judgement of the people who live there.

What would make us wrong

An arc is a hypothesis, and a hypothesis should name what would break it. If a single funder's money were to carry more than a part of any year, we would have repeated the mistake that closed the civic organisations of chapter 3. If a government asked for a score of its residents as the price of a pilot, the pilot would not happen. If a lender wanted a number instead of a file, we would offer the file and the statistics and nothing else. If a vote on a hub could be bought, we would have failed to make evidence weigh as much as the ballot. If the operators' association did not form by the fourth operator, growth would wait for it. Each of these is a pause, not a pivot, and each would be recorded with its date and its author in the open, as every decision behind this book is.

The open square

Five years from now the three squares of the mark (chapter 4) will be fuller, in more languages and more places. The fourth will still be open. It is not ours to fill.

Five years, five courses. The fourth square stays open.
