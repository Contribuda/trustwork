The experience, person by person

This is the system as it will be, told from the chair of each person who uses it: what they come for, what their one agent does for them under mandates they can read, and what stays theirs to decide. It is written as the product, not as a status list; chapter 18 says which of this exists. Where the civic ring or the financing ring appears, it is a direction of this book, not yet built, and said to be.

One door, one agent, one record

Everyone arrives the same way: one account, one lifelong identity, one representative agent that acts only under mandates confirmed on the hub's own form, never from a sentence typed in a chat. A person is often several things at once, a site engineer who writes articles, mentors newcomers and renovates their own flat, so "homeowner" or "professional" is a lens for suggestions, never a role that grants anything. Rights come from participation grants, company authority, staff roles and mandates, and from nothing else. What holds for everyone is listed at the end; here are the people.

The people who build

  • The self-builder

    Comes for a real plan for the house, the room or the roof they are about to build with their own money: phases, trades, permits, budget ranges with their basis, risks and decisions, in plain language; then the people the plan needs; later, borrowing and insuring stage by stage; and after completion a house record, the family's own, that continues through every repair and passes with the home to a buyer or an heir.

    The agent drafts the plan from a conversation and the country pack, proposes consultations and tasks, reminds them before a hidden work is covered, compiles the build file when they choose to show it to a lender, and at the end proposes what to share with the next family.

    Theirs to decide: approving every plan version, accepting every stage, choosing who sees which item of the plan, whether to borrow at all and from whom, and whether anything of the project is ever shared. The agent never approves, never accepts and never shares.

  • The worker

    Comes for a record of what they can do that belongs to them and not to an employer: a profile, finished work confirmed by the client, certificates checkable by number and, in time, a passport that travels to the next hub. For many it is the first rung of formality.

    The agent keeps the profile accurate, asks the client once for a confirmation and reminds once more, never with an incentive, and finds the course or the assessment of prior learning that leads to the next trade.

    Theirs to decide: what is published, with recorded and revocable consent; which jobs to answer; whether to take the assessment. Nobody is made visible against their will.

  • The professional

    Comes for the engineer's, architect's or estimator's place in a project: the drawings reviewed, the hidden work measured, the cost range given before a vote, the licence shown as checked on a date by the hub. And for being found by the client who needs them, for reasons the matching explains.

    The agent answers requests that fit, drafts proposals that are frozen the moment they are sent, keeps the calendar, prepares the feasibility note's template with the method the professional chose, and watches licence and insurance expiry.

    Theirs to decide: every professional judgement, under their own name and licence. The engine labels what the professional wrote as a professional estimate, different in appearance from a resident's idea and from any quote.

  • The expert and mentor

    Comes for consultations that pay and count: an advice session, a review of quotes, a site visit, a mentoring series for a newcomer.

    The agent keeps a versioned offering with the full price and the hub's commission shown before booking, schedules within window capacity, and brings the deliverable to the client for acceptance.

    Theirs to decide: what advice to give, and to whom. The hub's commission is the same for every expert in a category and never changes who is recommended.

  • The contractor

    Comes for work found fairly, quotes that cannot be undercut after the hour, an agreement both sides can prove identical, stage acceptances that protect them as much as the client, and a reputation from accepted work that explains itself and cannot be bought.

    The agent, or the company's one standing representative with a named accountable person, lays out the stages from the agreement, records work with dated, placed photographs, drafts the pay application against confirmed work records and the agreement's digest, proposes a remediation plan when a defect is recorded, and keeps the company's page claimed and current.

    Theirs to decide: the price, the bid, the remediation, and the public response to a fair negative observation, which they may dispute and never remove. Timing reputation measures them against the plan as it was agreed at the time, so a client's change is never their delay.

  • The supplier and manufacturer

    Comes for requests for quotes answered with frozen prices, a catalogue with immutable versions, confirmed deliveries, and the place on a plan step where their knowledge helps a builder choose and install correctly, because it fits, never because they paid.

    The agent watches the needs board for what the company sells, drafts quotes within mandates, records deliveries with evidence, and keeps installation guides and training labelled as the manufacturer's own.

    Theirs to decide: every quote and every promise. They can never buy a recommendation, a ranking or a position; a hub's open statistics will show how their systems perform by stage and defect category, and that is the only advertising the engine runs.

The people who commission with common money

  • The resident

    Comes for the street that floods, the stair that is missing, the roof that leaks: an idea, a place to discuss it, a costed note before the vote, and afterwards the record of what their vote became, with the budget line, the tender, the contractor, the photographs and the acceptance. The vote itself belongs to the municipality or a neutral keeper; the hub keeps the record around it.

    The agent knows from the country pack when the calendar opens and what right a resident is exercising when they attach a photograph, drafts the idea in their words, and offers to attach an observation to a public work's record, as "received" until the institution answers.

    Theirs to decide: whether to propose, whether to attend, how to vote, whether to observe, and whether their name appears at all. A resident is never the object of a record, and observations are pseudonymous by default.

  • The municipal official

    Comes for one record of what was promised, budgeted, contracted, built and accepted that does not reset at an election, with assemblies and committees configured from the law and the right of reply.

    The agent, the municipality's representative with a named accountable person, imports the approved budget's capital lines and links each to its project page, drafts replies to observations from the record, and prepares the yearly delivery report for a person to sign.

    Theirs to decide: the budget, the award, the reply, the payment through the municipality's own channels when the hub signals that a stage is accepted. Officials appear only as signatories of documents that are already public; the engine never rates an official as a person, and it never runs the vote.

  • The procurement officer

    Comes for a tender whose quotes are frozen once sent and sealed until the deadline, so that nobody undercuts late; approval routes where the requester never decides; and a private supplier base that nothing leaks from.

    The agent drafts the request from the engineer's note, checks the compliance matrix with citations, and lays out the comparison with stale versions excluded. Where the tender runs in the state's own system, the hub imports the notice as a sourced page and never rewrites it.

    Theirs to decide: the award, in their own session with step-up for money, and never by silence. Approval by expiry does not exist.

  • The auditor

    Comes for a public-works record that holds, side by side, the contractor's statement, the inspector's act and the residents' confirmation compared against a measurement, with a bill of quantities and a payment signal that can be traced to all three. An audit office's citizen-monitor programme finds here the structured place its monitors lacked.

    The agent reads what is public and what the institution shared, and drafts the gap between what the papers say and what the site shows.

    Theirs to decide: the finding. The engine takes none of an auditor's, a court's or a council's decisions; it gives each a record to work from.

The people who finance and insure

These three roles are introduced in chapter 10. Each is a reader the project owner adds or a participant with a narrow capability; none of them is Trustwork, and Trustwork is none of them.

  • The independent inspector

    Comes for a place where their act counts as what it is: a third record, separate from the builder's statement and the owner's acceptance, that closes a stage and lets a tranche move. And for a record of their own: acts filed on time, acts later contradicted by a confirmed defect, appeals heard.

    The agent schedules the visit when the builder's evidence arrives, drafts the act from the stage template, and files nothing until the person signs.

    Theirs to decide: what they saw. They read the shared stage and attach an act; they never edit the plan, never accept work and never see a budget the owner has not shared. Their liability is their professional law's, not the platform's.

  • The lender's credit officer

    Comes for a build file they can read in one format whichever hub it comes from: the approved plan, the estimate against the hub's ranges, the permits, the named professionals, the contractor's explained record, the stage gates, and afterwards every closed gate's three records. And for a hub's open statistics on what a stage costs and how long it takes in this region.

    The agent, on the lender's side, is the lender's own affair. On the owner's side, the agent compiles the file for the owner's review and sends it only when the owner adds the lender as a reader.

    Theirs to decide: the credit decision, in the lender's own systems and under its own licence. The engine records "submitted" and "decided" without reasons and composes the disbursement instruction only when a gate is closed; the lender or its escrow provider executes it. The lines of chapter 10 apply, among them that the engine never lends or holds money and that no payment changes what a family is shown.

  • The insurer's underwriter

    Comes for what a warranty provider sees during a build: who built, which stages were inspected by whom, which defects were found and fixed, what the structure is and where it stands, and a resilience rating if one exists. Over time, for defect frequencies by stage and building type from the hub's open statistics.

    The agent on the owner's side attaches the certificate of cover as a credential with an expiry, checked like a licence, on the project and, with consent, on the contractor's passport.

    Theirs to decide: the premium and the terms. The hub never ranks insurers or sells a policy (chapter 10).

The people who tell, teach and gather

  • The editor and the journalist

    Comes for a desk where every piece names its sources, corrections are new versions with a visible note, a second editor reviews, and the weekly magazine can follow one budget line into one building.

    The agent gathers sourced items, drafts summaries labelled as such, checks the source permissions before publication, and never publishes alone; a disclosed piece is released only under a named responsible person.

    Theirs to decide: what to publish, what to correct, and the sentence that explains why the drain matters. The by-line is a person's, and so is the correction.

  • The teacher and the training centre

    Comes for short practical courses with certificates checkable by number; the course for committee members on reading a bill of quantities and witnessing a hidden work; the course for a mason on the next trade; and, where an accredited body takes part, the assessment of prior learning recorded once and portably.

    The agent drafts course outlines from shared project knowledge, schedules assessments, issues the certificate the teacher approved, and keeps the accreditation shown only with its recorded source.

    Theirs to decide: who passed. A platform certificate is not a state qualification or licence, and the engine says so; a manufacturer's course is labelled as the manufacturer's.

  • The event organiser

    Comes for a conference, a site visit, a trade evening or a public hearing with registration, tickets through a licensed provider, check-in and partner meetings.

    The agent drafts the programme, handles registration within the mandate, and proposes partner meetings that both sides opt into.

    Theirs to decide: the programme, the price and who speaks. Attendance is counted as totals; nobody learns who sat where.

The people who run the hub

  • The hub operator

    Comes for running Trustwork for a country, a region or a city as a small company that takes legal responsibility, launches a team of operator agents across the twenty-three functions of a hub operating company, and adds people as the hub grows.

    The agents of the operating company work under operator grants, each with a named accountable person: the chief of staff prepares the daily brief, the editorial roles draft, support and trust-and-safety roles triage, finance drafts the monthly close with the operator's own accountant, compliance watches the gates. None of them passes a launch gate, enables a real provider, sets a score or reads private data.

    Theirs to decide: the country pack's facts with their sources, commissions as versioned and disclosed settings, the legal documents counsel approved, and the refusal to build what the charter forbids, even when a government asks.

What holds for everyone

What holds for everyone are the values of chapter 1: one lifelong identity; one agent under mandates the person can read, change and revoke, with the stop control one click away; private stays private; nothing for sale, no paid leads, and commissions that never change who is shown; sourced, claimed and verified always distinct; every automated limit explained and appealable to a different person; no resident ever scored; restricted places never served.

The agent prepares. The person decides. The record remembers.
