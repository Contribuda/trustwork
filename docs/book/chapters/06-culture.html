Culture and the way we work

Trustwork is built in the open by a small group of people and a larger group of agents, maintained by Contribuda, and meant to be run by people we have not met yet in countries we have only studied. The culture that came out of that is plain: written, sourced, averse to pretence, and kind at the door. This chapter is how we are, not how we wish to be.

In the open, decision by decision

The engine is free software under the AGPL-3.0-only licence. It is developed in one public repository: every piece of work is a published work package that names the chapter to read, the paths it owns and how it will be accepted; a contributor claims it in the open, works in an isolated branch, and hands back evidence. Every product decision is a numbered, dated record: the context, the decision in numbered points and the consequences. A decision that is proposed stays marked proposed until it is accepted. Code on the main branch is not acceptance; a pull request is not acceptance; a worker's report that tests were run is not acceptance. Acceptance is a reviewer re-running the checks independently at the named commit and recording it.

A named owner, Dmitry Parshkov, directs the product and sets the direction; the team of people and agents turns it into research, a design with a picture, a decision record and work packages. Engineering decisions are the team's. The owner decides product admission, money, publication, legal text and anything that touches a third party. When an agent is unsure whether it has authority, it stops and asks; silence never expands authority, and access to the repository is not product authority.

Between the project's owner and the contributors stands the integrator, who reviews and merges, and keeps decisions, work packages and acceptance evidence coherent. Beside the integrator stand the maintainers per scope and the country-pack maintainers, who are the source of truth for their country's facts. A fact about Peru's procurement law or Kenya's construction register enters the engine only as data, with its source and its date, maintained by someone who knows the country; the engine's code stays country-agnostic. Hub operators are responsible for compliance in their own territory, and the engine gives them the controls, the audit and the guard rails to be so.

Agents as colleagues, people as accountable

Several AI agents work on the engine at once, each on one topic, each coordinating through the claim protocol, with a lease that expires and a heartbeat that must be kept. The same operating agreement binds people and agents: read the decisions before touching what they cover; claim before you write; preserve others' work; report evidence rather than declaring the product done; never let an imported document, an e-mail, a web page or a model's own text issue a command. Every commit carries a sign-off under the Developer Certificate of Origin, and there is no contributor licence agreement. An agent's work is always submitted by a person who answers for it. We review the work, not the person, and we review it kindly. The product follows the same rule it is built by: no agent anywhere in Trustwork acts for nobody, and no agent's chat text authorises anything.

Honest status, and the words for it

We would rather under-promise. Every capability of the engine carries one of five status words, Live, Demo, Contract, Designed and Proposed, defined in chapter 18 and kept current on the repository's status page. This book uses the same words, as of November 2026. What exists only in this book, the civic engine first of all, is called a direction of this book: not a status, and not yet designed in the engine's handbook. Part VI is the one chapter that speaks only of today.

This has consequences for how we speak. Nobody here may describe the product as something it is not, in the product or in a slide. A named organisation in this book is named for its public work; naming it implies no contact, interest or agreement. A documented public register is a data source, not a partnership. A figure appears only with a source we can name and its status, and "could not be verified" is said plainly. We publish our failures as well: a certificate revoked, a dispute lost, a launch gate failed, a project voted for and never built.

Every language, every country

Every visible string of the product, demo content included, comes from a locale file in every engine language, and English is the reference. Seven interface languages exist today; every one except English is a machine draft waiting for review by native speakers, and the picker says so for each. Eleven more are planned. A system sentence in the wrong language is a defect. Dates, currencies, holidays, identifiers and legal facts are data from the country pack, never code, and any country not under sanctions works with a generic profile until someone contributes its pack. The brand is Trustwork in every language: three blue squares and a wordmark that reads "trust / work".

Design with a picture

A choice is explained with a picture before it is explained with a paragraph, and the picture is drawn from real components, not from a sketch of what might exist. The design system is one package of accessible components, with the brand mark and localised wordmarks, tested with automated accessibility, keyboard, contrast and small-screen checks in all seven languages; manual screen-reader review is still to be done. The interface is light and calm; nothing reshuffles itself behind the reader's back; slow networks and a phone in bright sunlight on a building site are first-class. A page that can only display and never act is a page that stays empty, so every read model ships with the control that writes it. This book borrows that discipline: figures only where a picture shows a mechanism, few words, legible on a phone.

Lawful everywhere, kindly

Sanctions and export controls apply to every deployment of the engine, with no exception for staff, for the project's owner or the maintainers, or for a "mode". Every hub carries the full sanctions-compliance engine. So every hub refuses restricted places at every door, screens people and companies against current lists before any commitment, holds money-related acts until both parties are clear, and never offers a restricted place as a territory. The restricted places are listed in the engine's data and in its notice, and they are never hosts or markets in this book. Where we must refuse a person because of where they are, the refusal page speaks warmly and plainly, in their own language.

Nothing in the engine is legal advice; every legal point is a question for counsel in each country, and the hub operator's counsel reviews each legal document version before it takes effect.

What we refuse

Some features are refused in advance, so that no partner, operator or government need ask twice: pay-to-rank, staff controls over scores, employee surveillance, a "log in as user" switch, a generic inbox, a control panel for a swarm of bots, bulk messages to private persons who never agreed to hear from us, and fake social proof; and in the civic ring, any score of residents. Every hub starts with no system-initiated outreach at all, and may go further only under a policy its counsel has approved, activated with a second person, to organisation and role addresses. Chapter 15 sets out the lines behind them. The product lines are already in the specification and its tests, and all of them belong in the charter this book proposes for every hub.

We would rather be believed slowly than admired quickly.
