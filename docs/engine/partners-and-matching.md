# Partners and matching

Normative requirements: [Matching and partners](modules/matching-and-partners.md). Participants: [who uses what](participants/README.md).

Requirement R34 (with R05 discovery and R14). Status: directories with filters are **Demo**; discovery candidates and eligibility are **Contract** inside procurement; the needs board, explainable matching and introductions are **Proposed**.

The most valuable thing a gathering point can do is help the right people find each other: a client with a good contractor, a contractor with a reliable supplier, a designer with a manufacturer, two firms that together can bid for a bigger job. Matching is a core journey of the engine, and it must be explainable and fair.

## What gets matched

| Demand side | Supply side |
|---|---|
| **Open needs** published on a project or company page ("ventilated facade subcontractor, Lyon region, 2,400 m², start in March") | **Capability profiles** of companies and professionals: what they do, where, at what capacity, with which licences and certificates |
| **RFQs** (private, sent to chosen suppliers) | **Catalogue offerings**: goods, works, services and bundles with public fields |
| **Partner requests** ("looking for a distributor in Brazil", "looking for a design partner for public tenders in India") | **Partner offers** ("we install curtain walls, open to subcontracting") |
| **Event meeting profiles** | Event meeting profiles |

A **capability profile** is structured: categories from the hub taxonomy, works and materials, regions served (ISO 3166-2), capacity ranges, typical project sizes, licences and certifications checked by the hub, languages, availability windows. It is public only in the fields the company chooses.

## How matching works (Proposed)

1. **Hard eligibility** — rules that must hold, shown as passed, failed or unknown:
   - serves the region of the need;
   - holds the licence or permit the country requires for this kind of work (from the hub's licence checks);
   - sanctions screening is clear for both sides;
   - not excluded by the requester (for example "no companies I already work with").
2. **Candidate retrieval** — structured filters plus hybrid search over public capability profiles, catalogue fields and project portfolios ([Search and knowledge](search-and-knowledge.md)). Only data the requester may see is ever used.
3. **Fit features** — each with a value and an explanation: category overlap, similar past projects, capacity fit, distance, availability, response history on the platform, reputation dimensions **with their sample sizes**.
4. **Ranking with uncertainty** — a transparent, versioned method. Unknown is not bad: a new company without reviews is labelled "new, no reviews yet" and is not pushed to the bottom for that reason alone. Ties rotate so the same few firms do not always come first.
5. **Explanation** — every suggestion shows "why this match": criteria met, criteria not met, unknowns, and the method version. The requester can tune weights for their own search.

**What never influences matching**: payment, subscription plan, sponsorship, advertising, staff preference. A hub cannot sell positions. This is the same rule as reputation (R14), and it is checked by tests that compare rankings with and without paid entitlements.

## The needs board (Proposed)

Public open needs form a board per hub, filterable by category, region, size and date. A need links to its project and owner card. Interested parties can:

- **Express interest** (a lightweight signal the owner sees in a list), or
- **Request an introduction**, or
- respond to an RFQ if the owner publishes one.

Needs expire or are closed by the owner with an outcome ("filled", "cancelled", "postponed"), which keeps the board honest.

## Introductions

An introduction is a **double-opt-in** connection, never a cold message dump:

```text
requested → accepted | declined | expired
```

- The requester states purpose and context (which need, which offer, which event).
- The recipient sees the requester's public card and the purpose, then accepts or declines. Declines are private.
- Contact details and a conversation thread open **only after acceptance**; the thread belongs to the introduction context (no generic inbox).
- An accepted introduction can become an RFQ invitation, a project participation or a partner agreement.

### Quotas and anti-spam

- Each person has a rolling quota of open introduction requests, set by the hub; agents draw from the same quota.
- Repeated declines lower the quota temporarily; reports of spam go to moderation.
- Requests generated by an agent are labelled "sent by Anna's agent on her behalf".

### External candidates

Matching can show **sourced** companies that are not platform users, clearly labelled. Their presence in a public source does **not** permit bulk outreach. Contacting them goes through an explicit, lawful channel chosen by a person (or by an agent whose mandate allows that channel), within the hub's outreach rules and the country's direct-marketing law.

## Alerts

People and companies subscribe to new needs, partner requests or RFQ invitations that match their profile. Alerts are notifications with a reason ("matches your category Facade works in Lyon region"), with frequency control.

## Across the mesh (Proposed)

A need published on a city hub federates to the country hub; a company in another country can match it if both hubs peer at `syndicate` level and the need is marked for cross-border interest. Introductions and RFQs across nodes travel to the counterparty's home node ([Federation](federation.md#deals-across-nodes)). Each node screens counterparties before the first commercial message.

## Data model (Proposed)

| Entity | Key fields |
|---|---|
| `capability_profile` | party, categories, regions, capacity ranges, licences refs, languages, visibility per field, revision |
| `open_need` | owner party, project ref, categories, region, size, schedule, status, outcome, visibility |
| `partner_post` | party, kind (`looking_for`, `offering`), categories, regions, text, expiry |
| `match_run` | requester principal, subject (need, RFQ, post), method version, created_at |
| `match_candidate` | run, candidate party or sourced record, eligibility results, features with explanations, rank, uncertainty |
| `introduction` | requester, recipient, purpose, context ref, status, generated_by (person or agent with mandate ref) |
| `outreach_quota` | principal, window, limit, used |

## Measuring without surveillance

The hub sees aggregate, anonymised counts: needs published, introductions requested and accepted, needs filled. Nobody gets a list of who looked at whose profile.

## Invariants

- Matching uses only data the requester may see; private quotes, budgets and floors never feed it.
- Paid entitlements never change a candidate's rank.
- An introduction reveals contact details only after acceptance.
- Agent-generated requests are labelled and count against the principal's quota.
